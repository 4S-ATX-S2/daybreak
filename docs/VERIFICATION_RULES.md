# 05 — VERIFICATION RULES
Every rule below was written from a **real error caught in a published edition.**

1. **Never publish a figure you did not retrieve this session.** If two numbers travel together
   (Brent+WTI, high+low, cases+hospitalizations) **fetch both**; never infer the second.
2. **Never convert a forecast into an observation.** Check tense on same-day sources. The rule applies
   wherever the number is *reused*, not just where it's first stated.
3. **Don't borrow a number from one subject to characterise another.** (A citywide attendance
   projection is not this hotel's workload.)
4. **A caveat attached in one edition must survive into the next.**
5. **Never let trimming separate a number from its source reference.** Re-check after every trim pass.
6. **Local vs regional/zone: publish one, name the other, never blend or split the difference.**
   *Ed. 249 leaked the zone's wind figure into two local rows. Grep every figure the zone supplies.*
7. **Don't claim corroboration you didn't obtain.** Single-sourced means labelled single-sourced.
8. **Check the publication YEAR — and the DAY — on anything a date-keyed search returns.**
   *Four occurrences. Annual events are the worst: near-identical coverage every year.*
9. **Treat a fetch summary as a claim, not data.** Prompt for faithful transcription. If two reads
   disagree, publish the corroborated one and say so. **This applies to hazard blocks, not just tables** —
   the EWX page returned three different answers in one session on ed. 249.
10. **When a source changes its own baseline, publish the ratio and flag it.** **And the inverse:
    before flagging a price/count as a source conflict, compare the two sources' TIMESTAMPS.**
    *Ed. 249 flagged a Brent "direction conflict" that was just a Friday-morning quote vs Friday's close.*
11. **"Nothing was posted" is a claim about a feed, and feeds carry more than one kind of item.**
    Write *what kind* of thing wasn't posted ("no new incident release"). Check both City feeds separately.

12. **⚠️ Rule 9 applies to FORECASTS too — hardest when a figure sits near a decision threshold.**
    Before publishing a forecast number that crosses a named criterion (advisory thresholds, capacity
    limits, curfew hours, price triggers), read the **PRECEDING issuance** as well as the current one.
    If they straddle the threshold, **publish both values and choose neither.**
    *Ed. 250 asserted Labor Day's heat index as 109° and therefore above the ≥108° advisory criterion.
    The 11:42 PM issuance said **107°**; the 12:44 AM issuance said **109°**. The criterion sits between
    them. Rewritten to "unsettled 107–109°, straddling the line; build for 109°, re-check first thing —
    plan for Black, hope for Red."*
    **Corollary: the closer a figure is to a threshold, the more reads it earns.** 96° needs one read;
    107° against a 108° criterion needs three.

## The audit covers COMPARATIVES too
*first / most / highest / strongest / nth time / since ed. N* — each needs a source or a stated basis,
or it gets cut. Ed. 249 cut three of its own: "the eighth time", "the most chips ever carried",
"the quietest stretch since ed. 245."
Ed. 250 cut four more: "the largest day of the year for this block" (×2), "the strongest sign yet that
this outbreak is over", "the first positive grid information in weeks", "on every measure a larger event."
**The sweep has caught something in every edition it has run on. It is not optional.**

## Corrections
- **Before the 0600 issue:** fix in place, **no revision letter** — but still name it in the confidence note.
- **After issue:** "Corrections to this edition as first issued" clause.
- **A correction ≠ a revision.** A forecast that changed is a revision. A mistake is a correction.
  Calling a revision a correction erodes the clause; calling a correction a revision hides a mistake.
  *Ed. 250's 107/109 was a **correction**, not a revision: the earlier issuance existed at build time
  and was not consulted.*
