The Craft · A Daily Note on People & Presence

The check-in that isn't a question

Ten days of heat, back to 22 August, three of them verified at exactly a hundred — and today is the second quiet one. This is the shape of shift in which people fall over — not during the hard week, when the adrenaline is doing the carrying, but in the flat days just after it, when the body finally gets permission to notice. And the trouble with checking on someone is that "you all right?" is not a question, it is a formality, and everyone in this building has been trained since childhood to answer it with "yeah, fine." You get nothing. Worse, you have now used up the moment, and you both know it.

The version that works is specific and it does not ask for a verdict. Name the thing, not the feeling — you were on the drive from eleven to four on Thursday, that was the hottest one — because a fact is easy to confirm and a feeling is not. Ask about the next hour, not the week: have you eaten?, when's your break?, are you drinking anything besides coffee? Those get answered honestly because they are small enough to answer honestly. Say your own first if you want a real reply — people match what they are given, and a supervisor who admits to being tired makes it safe to be tired. And then the hard part: leave the silence alone. Most people say the true thing about four seconds after the polite thing, and only if nobody fills the gap. If you learn nothing else from this note, learn to wait through those four seconds.
