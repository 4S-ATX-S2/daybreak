The Craft · A Daily Note on People & Presence

The check-in that isn't a question. Ten days of heat, back to 22 August, three of them verified at exactly a hundred — and today is the second quiet one. This is the shape of shift in which people fall over — not during the hard week, when adrenaline does the carrying, but in the flat days after. The trouble is that "you all right?" is a formality, not a question, and everyone here was trained from childhood to answer it "yeah, fine" — you get nothing and you have used up the moment. The version that works is specific and asks for no verdict. Name the thing, not the feeling — you were on the drive eleven to four Thursday, that was the hottest one — because a fact is easy to confirm and a feeling is not. Ask about the next hour, not the week: have you eaten, when's your break, are you drinking anything besides coffee. Those get answered honestly because they are small enough to answer honestly. Say your own first — a supervisor who admits to being tired makes it safe to be tired. Then the hard part: leave the silence alone. Most people say the true thing about four seconds after the polite thing, and only if nobody fills the gap.
