The Craft · A Daily Note on People & Presence

The hour after it goes wrong. Something will go wrong this weekend — a festival on the bridge, a holiday, six days of heat and a full house. What is worth knowing is that guests do not remember the failure nearly as clearly as they remember the hour after it. By tomorrow the fault has blurred; the shape of what we did next is still sharp. Three things make that hour work, and none is a discount. Own it in the first sentence — not “unfortunately the system shows”, just “we got this wrong, and I'm sorry.” A person braced for a fight who doesn't get one puts it down; a person who has to prove the failure is still angry an hour later, and now at us. Then give them the next fact, not the next feeling — “twenty minutes” beats “as soon as possible” even when it is worse than they hoped, because a number can be planned around and a sentiment cannot; come back at nineteen and you have repaired something larger than the room. And close the loop yourself: the person who apologised is the person who should reappear. Last — do not make them tell it twice. Carrying the story to the desk, the manager and the next shift is the part guests actually notice, and it costs nothing but a sentence in the handover.

Readiness · Safety, Security & CrisisTopic 3 of 12 — Severe Weather & Shelter Points
Know This

In Central Texas the danger is lightning and fast water, far more often than wind. Lightning strikes up to ten miles from the rain you can see — which is why the rule is 30/30: if the gap between flash and thunder is thirty seconds or less, the storm is close enough to hit you, and you wait thirty full minutes after the last thunder before going back out. Shelter means a substantial building or a hard-topped vehicle. A cabana, a marquee, an umbrella and the pool deck are not shelter, however dry they keep you.

Do This Today

Find your two. From your own station, walk to the nearest interior refuge — an inside corridor or stairwell, away from glass — and then to the one you would use from the busiest place you work. Check both are unlocked and lit. If you work outside, count the seconds from your post to the nearest real door and remember the number. Two minutes, once, and it is yours for the season.

If It Happens

Clear the exterior first, and clear it by name. Pool deck, terraces, drive, banquet lawn — move people inside and away from glass, not into the lifts. Then Security Duty Ext 8240. Don't debate the sky with a guest: “we're moving inside for thirty minutes” ends the conversation and starts the clock — and the clock restarts on every thunderclap. First thirty seconds: inside, away from glass, thirty minutes.
